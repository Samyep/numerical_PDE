# Results: symmetric 2/4/6-cell interface stencils

Seed: 0. This is a controlled screening experiment, not yet a multi-seed
architecture claim.

## Question and controlled setup

The original network used five primitive-variable cells around an interface
and was asymmetric about that interface. This experiment replaces it with the
following symmetric inputs for the flux at `i+1/2`:

| Stencil | Primitive-state input |
|---:|---|
| 2 cells | `(P_i | P_{i+1})` |
| 4 cells | `(P_{i-1}, P_i | P_{i+1}, P_{i+2})` |
| 6 cells | `(P_{i-2}, P_{i-1}, P_i | P_{i+1}, P_{i+2}, P_{i+3})` |

The ablation was run independently for both retained methods:

1. HLLC + learned Roe-coordinate correction.
2. Central + nonnegative Roe dissipation + proposal-feasibility loss
   (`lambda_feas = 1e-3`).

Every arm used the same width (72), optimizer, seed, 580 periodic training
trajectories, 136 disjoint periodic validation trajectories, and a 50,000
update cap. The trajectories are generated by the existing strict 512-cell
Rusanov + SSP-RK2 reference and conservatively restricted to 64 cell
averages. Checkpoint selection uses validation rollout NRMSE only; none of the
512-cell periodic or nonperiodic deployment cases is used for selection.

The input-layer size changes with stencil size, so the trainable parameter
counts are 5,979 / 6,411 / 6,843 for 2 / 4 / 6 cells. Six cells therefore has
more capacity than four; the four-cell result is not explained by having the
largest network.

## Convergence

All six arms reached the preregistered validation-plateau stopping condition
before the 50,000-update cap.

| Method | Cells | Best update | Stop update | Validation rollout NRMSE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| HLLC + Roe correction | 2 | 6,700 | 11,700 | 0.0417391 |
| HLLC + Roe correction | **4** | 8,500 | 13,500 | **0.0308824** |
| HLLC + Roe correction | 6 | 8,300 | 12,300 | 0.0330483 |
| Central + nonnegative Roe + feasibility | 2 | 9,900 | 12,600 | 0.0434514 |
| Central + nonnegative Roe + feasibility | **4** | 14,800 | 18,800 | **0.0305662** |
| Central + nonnegative Roe + feasibility | 6 | 10,900 | 13,900 | 0.0332319 |

Thus four cells wins validation in both model families. This conclusion does
not depend on stopping at a shared small training budget.

## Periodic zero-shot deployment at 512 cells

The reference is native HLLC-2048, restricted to 512 cell averages only for
the error metric. Native HLLC-512 has mean rollout NRMSE `0.0742369`.

| Method | Cells | Mean rollout NRMSE | Reduction vs HLLC-512 | Normalized final TV excess | Excess extrema |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| HLLC + Roe correction | 2 | 0.0686599 | 7.51% | 0.9844 | 209 |
| HLLC + Roe correction | **4** | **0.0565859** | **23.78%** | 0.1365 | 60 |
| HLLC + Roe correction | 6 | 0.0707346 | 4.72% | **0.0652** | **43** |
| Central + nonnegative Roe + feasibility | 2 | 0.0648347 | 12.67% | 1.0057 | 270 |
| Central + nonnegative Roe + feasibility | **4** | **0.0500695** | **32.55%** | 0.1781 | 86 |
| Central + nonnegative Roe + feasibility | 6 | 0.0565290 | 23.85% | **0.0882** | **66** |

Four cells gives the best error in both families. The two-cell solutions have
obvious high-frequency shock-region oscillations; their TV excess is about
one full reference TV and their excess-extrema counts are 209/270. Six cells
is smoother than four by these diagnostics, but its rollout error is higher.
For the central six-cell arm, the periodic near-vacuum minimum pressure is
`1.00117e-5`, just above the `1e-5` safety floor, so its smoothness is not a
reason to prefer it.

## Zero-shot transmissive nonperiodic deployment at 512 cells

These checkpoints were trained only on periodic data. For nonperiodic tests,
the NN is used only at interior interfaces with replicated ghost cells; both
physical boundary fluxes remain analytic. The reference is strict
nonperiodic HLLC-2048. Native HLLC-512 has eight-case mean rollout NRMSE
`0.0512534`.

| Method | Cells | All 8 cases | Five centered cases | Three boundary-interaction cases | Centered TV excess | Centered excess extrema |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| HLLC + Roe correction | 2 | 0.0373148 | 0.0423955 | **0.0288470** | 0.5407 | 94 |
| HLLC + Roe correction | **4** | **0.0351071** | **0.0336160** | 0.0375922 | 0.0593 | 31 |
| HLLC + Roe correction | 6 | 0.0455521 | 0.0462572 | 0.0443768 | **0.0357** | **25** |
| Central + nonnegative Roe + feasibility | 2 | **0.0323964** | 0.0367174 | **0.0251948** | 0.4463 | 94 |
| Central + nonnegative Roe + feasibility | **4** | 0.0369023 | **0.0325591** | 0.0441408 | 0.0875 | 46 |
| Central + nonnegative Roe + feasibility | 6 | 0.0396094 | 0.0380669 | 0.0421803 | **0.0515** | **35** |

The apparently strong two-cell boundary score must not be interpreted as a
general win: away from boundary interaction, the same model has much larger
TV excess and many more spurious extrema. A plausible explanation is that a
two-cell interface input does not expose the model to several replicated
ghost cells near a boundary, but this experiment does not isolate that cause.
The result motivates a future *boundary-local* two-cell fallback; it does not
support replacing the global stencil by two cells.

For the general interior operator, four cells gives the best centered-case
accuracy in both families while reducing the severe two-cell oscillation.
Six cells again trades lower TV/extrema for substantially worse error.

## Comparison with the legacy asymmetric five-cell input

The old five-cell checkpoints remain loadable and were not silently changed.
A cross-experiment comparison gives:

| Method and metric | Legacy 5 cells | Symmetric 4 cells | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| HLLC + Roe validation | 0.0315568 | **0.0308824** | 4 cells 2.14% lower |
| HLLC + Roe periodic 512 | **0.0562689** | 0.0565859 | effectively tied; 4 cells 0.56% higher |
| HLLC + Roe nonperiodic 512 | **0.0347194** | 0.0351071 | effectively tied; 4 cells 1.12% higher |
| Central-feasibility validation | 0.0320910 | **0.0305662** | 4 cells 4.75% lower |
| Central-feasibility periodic 512 | 0.0540494 | **0.0500695** | 4 cells 7.36% lower |
| Central-feasibility nonperiodic 512 | **0.0368331** | 0.0369023 | effectively tied; 4 cells 0.19% higher |

The defensible conclusion is therefore not that four cells universally beats
five. It removes the arbitrary left bias with no material loss and improves
validation plus the central model's periodic deployment, so it is the cleaner
default architecture.

## Scientific-integrity checks

`audit_results.py` independently reloads every checkpoint and verifies:

- the recorded validation minimum and update match the saved learning curve;
- every arm stopped by convergence rather than the cap;
- parameter counts match the intended stencil;
- the learned proposal is nonzero (proposal-change relative RMS is
  `0.0678`--`0.0734`, rather than collapsing to the classical flux);
- the nonperiodic padded feature indexing matches periodic interior indexing
  bit-for-bit; raw flux differences are at most `2.384e-7`, consistent with
  float32 matrix-multiplication rounding;
- no circular wrap leakage occurs, analytic boundary fluxes are exact,
  constant states are preserved, and the finite-volume conservation identity
  holds;
- all reported periodic and nonperiodic rollouts remain admissible and pass
  the hard-projected entropy checks.

The audit status is `pass` with no failures.

## Decision

Use the symmetric **four-cell** input as the default for both retained model
families. Keep six cells as a smooth/over-conservative ablation, and keep two
cells only as evidence for a future boundary-local design. Do not claim final
universality until the four-cell choice is repeated over additional training
seeds and boundary conditions.
