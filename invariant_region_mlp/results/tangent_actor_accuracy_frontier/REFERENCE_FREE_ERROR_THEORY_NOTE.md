# Reference-Free Error Control for Tangent-Actor

Let (Y=(u,z)), (A=(r,q)), and

\[
L_A(Y)=f(A)+Df(A)^\top(Y-A).
\]

For a fixed sufficiently regular Actor (A), let (v^A) solve

\[
v_t^A+\Delta v^A+L_A(v^A,\sqrt2\nabla v^A)=0,
\qquad v^A(T,\cdot)=g,
\]

and write (Y^A=(v^A,\sqrt2\nabla v^A)). Substitution gives the exact nonlinear residual

\[
R_A=f(Y^A)-L_A(Y^A).
\]

If (f\in C^2) on a convex neighborhood containing every line segment between (A(t,x)) and (Y^A(t,x)), and its joint Hessian has operator norm at most (H), Taylor's theorem gives

\[
|R_A(t,x)|\le \frac H2\lVert Y^A(t,x)-A(t,x)\rVert^2.
\]

## A deterministic PDE stability bound

Assume the nonlinear terminal-value problem has a unique bounded classical solution (u^*\in C^{1,2}), (v^A\in C^{1,2}), and (f) is Lipschitz on the attained solution range with constants (L_u,L_z). Assume also that the interpolated drift generated by (f_z) is bounded, or satisfies conditions sufficient for the corresponding Girsanov density to be a true martingale. Linearizing (f(Y^A)-f(Y^*)) along the segment between the two solutions gives bounded coefficients (a,b), with (|a|\le L_u) and (lVert b\rVert\le L_z). The difference (w=v^A-u^*) has zero terminal data and solves a linear parabolic equation forced by (R_A).

The parabolic comparison principle, equivalently a Feynman–Kac representation under the bounded drift, yields

\[
\lVert v^A-u^*\rVert_\infty
\le C_T\lVert R_A\rVert_\infty,
\qquad
C_T=
\begin{cases}
(e^{L_uT}-1)/L_u,&L_u>0,\\
T,&L_u=0.
\end{cases}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\lVert v^A-u^*\rVert_\infty
\le \frac{C_TH}{2}
\lVert Y^A-A\rVert_\infty^2.
\]

This is a conditional a posteriori result. It requires global control of the fixed-Actor solution, its Gradient, the coefficient range, and the sup-norm fixed-point defect. Bounded Hessian alone does not establish existence, uniqueness, or stability.

## What the sampled consistency score certifies

The implemented score estimates a normalized mean-square defect on a finite iid state bank. Two independent Monte Carlo label replications permit subtraction of the sampling variance of their average. For example, suppose the exact squared defect (D(S)^2) is bounded by (B^2), the states (S_i) are iid from (\mu), and exact fixed-Actor labels are available. Hoeffding's inequality gives, with probability at least (1-\delta),

\[
\mathbb E_\mu D(S)^2
\le \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n D(S_i)^2
+B^2\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{2n}}.
\]

For (m) predeclared Actors evaluated on the same independent bank, replacing (\delta) by (\delta/m) gives simultaneous coverage by a union bound. With Monte Carlo labels, an additional confidence bound for the independent label noise must be added; the two-replication subtraction used by the experiment is an unbiased noise correction, but a rigorous confidence bound further requires bounded or sub-exponential path payoffs. Alternatively, checkpoint selection and certification must use independent banks. These assumptions yield a population (L^2(\mu)) statement only.

That statement does not imply a global sup-norm bound. A conversion to (L^\infty) would additionally require a compact domain, a known Lipschitz constant for the defect, and a state bank forming a sufficiently fine covering. If the fill distance is (h) and the defect is (K)-Lipschitz, then

\[
\lVert Y^A-A\rVert_\infty
\le \max_i\lVert Y^A(S_i)-A(S_i)\rVert+Kh.
\]

Obtaining a probabilistic bound on (h) requires a sampling density bounded away from zero on that compact domain, and its sample complexity is exponential in the ambient dimension. The present Gaussian, unbounded state distribution and finite bank do not meet those conditions. Neural-network Lipschitz upper bounds would also be far too loose to make this route useful here.

The reference-free fixed-point score is therefore an empirically useful checkpoint selector and stability diagnostic. It is not a formal PDE-error certificate, and the numerical results do not interpret it as one.
