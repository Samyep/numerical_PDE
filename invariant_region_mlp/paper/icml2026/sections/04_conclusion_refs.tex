\section{Conclusion}
MLP can amplify Monte Carlo noise when infeasible intermediate states are passed through nonlinear generators. Constraint-aware MLP inserts a certified retraction exactly at that interface. The strongest result is structural: on a published dimensionality counterexample, enforcing the PDE-certified active subspace removes all nonlinear corrections pathwise and changes the error from ambient-dimension dependent to the exact dimension-free rate $(4-2/\pi)/N$. Beyond that example, high-dimensional experiments show large reductions in generator error and recursive variance, while samplewise and batchwise retractions expose a practical bias--variance tradeoff. The broader message is modest: if PDE structure already tells us where the exact state can live, using that information inside the stochastic recursion can be materially safer than waiting until the final output.
